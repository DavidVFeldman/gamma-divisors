Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed paper, "Divisors in windows and the
Euler-Mascheroni constant," for publication in [JOURNAL].

The paper studies the average number of divisors of an integer q lying in
an interval whose endpoints move with q, and separates two regimes. When
both endpoints run along fixed powers of q, the averages obey a law from
which Euler's constant is absent, and the average counts for two windows
stand in the ratio of the windows' exponent lengths. When a window anchors
at a growing threshold and its square root, the logarithms cancel instead
and the average discrepancy equals Euler's constant exactly. Windows on
the scale of q itself interpolate between the regimes; there the limiting
constant is shown to depend on an endpoint convention, the two candidate
constants differing precisely when the reciprocal of the scale parameter
is an integer. The paper also isolates a logarithm-free approximation to
Euler's constant converging at the rate (2/3)n^{-2}, and evaluates the
mean of the scaled-window constants as zeta(2) - 1.

One statement proved here has a history worth noting. The assertion that
an integer has, on average, twice as many divisors in (n^{1/4}, n^{1/2})
as in (n^{1/8}, n^{1/4}) follows from two applications of the
Euler-constant interpretation, yet mentions no transcendental constant;
the question of a proof that never passes through Euler's constant was
posed publicly in October 2008 (arXiv:0810.1354) and has, to my
knowledge, remained unaddressed since. The window law of Section 3
answers it, and shows the phenomenon to be general.

All arguments are elementary and self-contained. The principal results
carry machine verification in Lean 4 against the Mathlib library: the
counting identity on which the method rests, the fixed-window law, the
main theorem in the instance F(q) = sqrt(q), the scaled-window theorem
at alpha = 1/2 in both endpoint conventions, the vanishing of the window
difference, and the rate of convergence of the logarithm-free
approximation. Each depends only on the three axioms of Lean's standard
foundation. The companion source is archived at
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21909843 and cited in the paper.

The paper is not under consideration elsewhere.
